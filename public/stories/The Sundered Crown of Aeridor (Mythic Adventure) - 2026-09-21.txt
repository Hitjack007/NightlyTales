The Sundered Crown of Aeridor
=============================

Genre: Mythic Adventure
Date: 2026-09-21
Words: 4,924

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The wind that scoured the Jagged Peaks usually sang a song of ancient ice and granite, a keen melody that sharpened the mind. Today, it carried a different tune: the dry rasp of dying things, the sour reek of a world unraveling. Kael huddled low amidst a cluster of petrified ash-trees, their skeletal branches clawing at a sky bruised purple and grey. Below him, the Valley of Whispers, once a verdant ribbon of life, was a stretch of withered husks and dust.

A sound like tearing silk rippled through the air, followed by a guttural roar that vibrated through Kael's very bones. He peered through a crack in the twisted bark. In the heart of the blighted valley, a monstrous shadow writhed. It was the Ghast-Wyrm, a creature of legend, said to dwell in the underworld’s deepest fissures, roused only by cataclysm. Its scales, usually the obsidian sheen of volcanic glass, were dulled and flaking, oozing a sickly phosphorescence. Around it, the ground steamed, not with heat, but with a cold decay that sucked the very essence from the earth.

Before the Wyrm stood a figure Kael knew by reputation: Lyra of the Iron Will, a warrior whose deeds were sung in distant halls. She wore the burnished bronze of the Sun Knights, her shield bearing the emblem of the Radiant Star, her sword, Dawn’s Edge, a blade fabled to cut through shadow. Lyra moved with a dancer’s grace, deflecting the Wyrm’s venomous spittle, her war cry echoing with defiant courage. But even as she pressed her attack, Kael saw the tell-tale shimmer around her – the encroaching tendrils of the blight, coiling around her limbs, sucking the vitality from her armor, dimming the light from Dawn's Edge.

The Ghast-Wyrm lunged, a gaping maw lined with teeth like shattered obsidian. Lyra met it, her shield blazing briefly, but the blight-born creature was too strong, too steeped in the rot that consumed the land. With a sickening crunch, the Wyrm’s head snapped down, crushing Lyra beneath its colossal weight. The last thing Kael saw was the faint, desperate flicker of light from Dawn's Edge before it, too, was extinguished, swallowed by the encroaching shadows.

A cold dread, more profound than any Kael had ever known, seized him. Lyra, the mighty, the celebrated, had fallen. And her quest – to slay the Ghast-Wyrm and halt the spread of the Great Blight – had ended in utter ruin. Kael had come to these peaks, not as a hero, but as a desperate seeker of ancient herbs, hoping to find some remedy for his younger sister, Elara, who lay wasting in their village, her skin greyed, her breath shallow, mirroring the blight's creep across the land. Now, even that meager hope seemed a fool’s dream. The blight was not merely a sickness; it was a consuming force, an unmaking.

He turned and fled, the desolate wind his only companion, the image of Lyra’s defeat burned into his mind. He ran through the dwindling twilight, down the treacherous mountain paths, driven by a terrible urgency. He had to reach Aeridor, their village, before the sun rose again, before the blight claimed another.

Aeridor, nestled in the protective crook of the Whisperwind River, was a ghost of its former self. The river, once a lively torrent, now flowed sluggishly, its waters murky and still. The ancient oak, under which generations had gathered, stood leafless, its bark peeling like old parchment. Smoke from hearth fires was thin, acrid, tasting of fear.

Kael found his way to their small, wattled hut. Inside, the air was heavy with the scent of sickness and the dying embers of a charcoal brazier. His mother, Elara, sat by the pallet where his sister lay, her face etched with despair. Elara’s skin, usually sun-kissed and vibrant, was a pale, mottled grey, her eyes sunken, her breath a fragile whisper.

"Kael, you're back," his mother murmured, her voice hollow. "Did you find anything? The sunstone moss? The river-willow root?"

He shook his head, the words catching in his throat. "Mother, it’s no use. I saw... I saw the Ghast-Wyrm. Lyra of the Iron Will, she tried to fight it. She failed. The blight... it's stronger than any mortal hand."

His mother’s gaze drifted to Elara. "Then what hope is there for us? For her?"

Kael sank onto a stool, the weight of his failure pressing down on him. He remembered the elder, Master Borin, speaking in hushed tones of the old legends, of a time before the blight, when the land flourished under the favor of the gods. He spoke of the Sundered Crown of Aeridor, a relic of immense power, shattered millennia ago during a divine war. Its fragments, scattered across the realms, were said to hold the very essence of creation and life.

"There is an old tale," Kael began, his voice barely a whisper. "Master Borin used to speak of it. The Sundered Crown. They say it was broken by the Shadow Lord in the First Age, and its pieces scattered across the nine realms. But that its re-forging would heal the deepest wounds, even those inflicted by the gods themselves."

His mother looked at him, a flicker of something, perhaps madness, perhaps desperate hope, in her eyes. "A child’s fable, Kael. For generations, the elders have spoken of it. None have ever found even a single shard. Many have tried, many have died."

"Lyra died trying to fight the *symptom*," Kael countered, "not the cause. What if the blight is a wound, a scar from the Sundered Crown’s breaking, festering for ages, and now finally consuming us? What if the only way to heal it is to re-forge the crown?"

He rose, a cold resolve settling in his gut. He was no warrior, no renowned hero. He was a hunter, a gatherer of roots and berries, skilled with a bow and quick of foot, but a blade felt alien in his hand. Yet, watching Elara's fading light, Kael knew he had no choice. He would face the mythical beasts, the divine trials, the impossible quests. He would seek the Sundered Crown.

The next morning, Kael stood before Master Borin, the oldest and wisest of Aeridor’s remaining elders. Borin’s face was a roadmap of wrinkles, his eyes milky with age, but they held a spark of ancient knowledge.

"So, the tales have finally captured your heart, young Kael," Borin rasped, his voice like dry leaves rustling. "Many have heard the legends, few have ever understood their true meaning. The Sundered Crown is not merely a relic of power. It is a covenant, a bond between the earth and the heavens, broken by hubris and greed."

Borin led Kael to a hidden chamber beneath the village's communal hall, a place Kael had never known existed. The air was thick with dust and the scent of forgotten incense. On a stone altar lay a scroll, brittle with age, covered in symbols Kael did not recognize.

"This is the Chant of the Sundered Star," Borin explained, tracing a gnarled finger over the scroll. "It speaks of the Crown's true name, Aeridor, and its fragments. There are three shards. The first, the Tear of Sol, lies within the Sunken City of Eldoria, guarded by the Silent Sentinels. The second, the Heart of Gaea, rests in the embrace of the Root-Mother, deep within the Whispering Wood. The third, the Eye of Aether, is held within the highest aerie of the Storm-Lords, atop the unforgiving peaks of the Sky-Spires."

Borin looked at Kael, his gaze piercing. "Each fragment holds a piece of the Crown’s essence. Sol, the life-giving warmth. Gaea, the fertile earth. Aether, the boundless sky. Re-uniting them... it will not be enough to simply collect them. The Crown must be re-forged in the Heart of Creation itself, the legendary Forge of Hephaestus, said to reside in the Underworld, beyond the Veil of Shadows."

Kael felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. The Underworld. Hephaestus, the divine smith, whose fires burned with the fury of creation and destruction. His quest was even more impossible than he had imagined. But the image of Elara’s fading breath steeled his resolve.

"Tell me what I must do," he said, his voice firm.

Borin gave him a small, tarnished silver amulet – the emblem of their village, a simple river stone – and a weathered map, its edges frayed. "This amulet, it bears the blessing of the River Spirit, Sela. It may offer you protection against the currents of the Underworld. And this map... it marks the known paths, and the rumored ones, to these places. Beware, Kael. The blight has twisted more than just the land. It has warped minds and corrupted creatures. Trust your instincts, and the spirit of Aeridor within you."

Kael bid his mother and Elara a silent farewell, promising in his heart to return. He took his hunting bow, a quiver of arrows, a hunter's knife, and the meager provisions he could gather. He was no warrior, but he would become one. He would find the Sundered Crown.

His first destination, the Sunken City of Eldoria, lay across the treacherous Mire of Whispers, a marshland where the blight had taken root most fiercely. The air was thick and humid, filled with the buzzing of unseen insects and the croaking of mutated amphibians. The ground beneath his feet was a spongy mass of decaying flora, releasing putrid gases that burned his throat.

As he ventured deeper, the mist grew denser, swirling around ancient, half-submerged ruins – the remnants of a civilization lost to time. These were the outskirts of Eldoria. He navigated by Borin’s map and the faint, phosphorescent glow of blight-born fungi. The further he went, the more he heard it: a low, mournful singing, like wind chimes made of glass, but utterly devoid of joy. It was the song of the Silent Sentinels.

He finally arrived at the edge of what must have been the city's main plaza. Skeletal spires of ancient marble pierced the mist, draped in thick, black moss. In the center, a colossal, broken statue of a winged goddess lay submerged, her face eaten away by time and blight. Guarding it were three figures, tall and slender, their forms cloaked in what appeared to be woven water and shimmering algae. These were the Silent Sentinels, beings of pure water-spirit, twisted by the blight into melancholic guardians. They stood motionless, their silent song weaving a web of despair that threatened to drown Kael’s resolve.

As he approached, one Sentinel turned, its head – a smooth, featureless orb – tilting towards him. No eyes, no mouth, yet Kael felt an overwhelming sadness radiating from it. The song intensified, seeking to seep into his soul, to make him lay down and join the blighted tranquility. He fought it, focusing on Elara’s face, on the image of Aeridor restored.

He remembered Borin’s words: "The Sentinels guard with sorrow, not with force. To pass them, one must offer a piece of their own heart, not their blade." Kael reached into his pouch and pulled out the small, polished river stone, a gift from Elara when she was well, a symbol of their home. It was a tangible piece of his heart. He laid it gently on the cracked pedestal before the Sentinel.

The water-being rippled, its song faltered, replaced by a sigh that resonated with ancient pain. It stretched out a translucent hand, not to take the stone, but to point. Beyond the broken statue, a passage opened in the mist, leading down into the deeper waters. The Sentinels did not move to stop him. He had offered his heart, and they had seen its purity.

He plunged into the chill, murky waters. The amulet of Sela glowed faintly, pushing back the immediate darkness, allowing him to see. He swam through submerged archways, past crumbling frescoes depicting a glorious past, until he reached a cavernous chamber. In its center, pulsating with a soft, golden light, lay a shard of what could only be the Crown: a teardrop-shaped gem, warm and vibrant, radiating the very essence of sunlight. The Tear of Sol.

As he reached for it, a serpentine shadow detached itself from the ceiling. A Daggertooth Eel, its scales black as night, its eyes glowing with malevolent intelligence, its teeth like honed razors. It was a guardian, fierce and relentless. Kael, still burdened by his hunter’s instinct, not a warrior’s, drew his knife.

The eel was swift, darting through the water, its tail lashing. Kael dodged, feeling the rush of water as it narrowly missed him. He knew he couldn't outfight it in its element. He remembered the Silent Sentinels, their sorrow, his gift. He closed his eyes, remembering Elara, the blight, the desperation. He poured his fear, his hope, his love for his sister into the river amulet. It flared, not with defensive power, but with a raw, resonant emotion.

The Daggertooth Eel, accustomed to preying on fear and weakness, recoiled from this unexpected surge of pure, desperate emotion. It sensed the pain, but also the unwavering resolve. It didn't understand. Confusion warred with aggression in its eyes. Kael saw his chance. He didn't attack. Instead, he reached out, not for the eel, but for the Tear of Sol. As his fingers closed around the warm gem, the cavern was flooded with golden light. The Daggertooth Eel, startled and disoriented by the sudden brightness, fled into the deeper shadows.

With the Tear of Sol clutched tightly, Kael swam back to the surface, the light of the gem illuminating his path. He had faced sorrow and primal fear, and emerged with the first shard.

His next journey led him to the Whispering Wood, a forest renowned for its ancient, sentient trees and the elusive Root-Mother. The blight had touched even this sacred grove, causing some trees to weep black sap, their leaves crumbling to dust. But deeper within, life still pulsed, albeit faintly. Kael followed the faint currents of magical energy, guided by Borin’s map and the Tear of Sol, which hummed softly in his pouch.

The air grew heavy with the scent of damp earth and verdant decay. The trees here were unlike any he had ever seen – colossal titans, their trunks wider than houses, their branches reaching for the sky like gnarled arms. Their roots, thick as anacondas, snaked across the forest floor, forming a natural labyrinth. He could feel eyes on him, not hostile, but watchful. The very trees seemed to observe his progress.

After days of navigating the dense, echoing wood, he stumbled upon a clearing. In its center, nestled amongst roots that rose like petrified waves, was a grotto, its entrance draped with luminous vines. He knew this was the domain of the Root-Mother.

He entered, the air inside warm and moist, alive with the rustle of unseen things and the gentle drip of water. Before him, intertwined with the living roots of a colossal oak, was the Root-Mother herself. She was not a single being, but a conglomeration of organic matter: bark, moss, vines, and leaves, forming a vaguely humanoid shape of immense size. Her eyes were pools of ancient amber, filled with quiet wisdom and immense suffering. From her colossal form, tendrils of blight slowly spread, consuming patches of her sacred grove even as she tried to fight it. She was the heart of the forest, and the blight was consuming her from within.

"You seek the Heart of Gaea," her voice rumbled, not through sound, but directly into his mind, resonating with the very earth. "Many have come. Many have sought. But few understand its true cost."

Kael knelt, bowing his head in reverence. "Great Root-Mother, my sister is dying, my village fades. The blight consumes all. I seek to re-forge the Sundered Crown, to heal this ravaged land."

"The Heart of Gaea is not a prize to be taken," she resonated. "It is a trust. To claim it, you must offer an act of selfless nurturing, a healing to that which has been forgotten, or face the blight’s true despair."

Kael looked around. The grotto, while beautiful, held signs of the blight's slow creep. A patch of ancient, moss-covered stone was turning black, its vibrant green fading. A small, sapling-like root near the Root-Mother’s base was withered, barely clinging to life, its tiny leaves brown and brittle.

He had no powerful magic, no divine artifacts of healing. All he had was his hunter’s knowledge of the earth, his respect for life. He spent hours, not seeking the Heart of Gaea, but tending to the blighted sapling. He carefully scraped away the black, corrupted moss from the stone, exposing the pure rock beneath. He searched for uncorrupted soil, for rare, vibrant fungi that still held life, and painstakingly replanted them around the sapling, whispering words of encouragement, willing life into it. He knew it was a small, almost futile gesture against the vastness of the blight, but it was all he could give.

As he worked, a shimmering green light began to emanate from his hands, a faint echo of the Tear of Sol’s golden glow. The sapling, minuscule as it was, began to unfurl a fresh, vibrant green leaf. The patch of stone he had cleansed seemed to hum with renewed energy.

The Root-Mother's immense form rippled. "You have understood," her voice echoed, softer this time. "The true cost is not strength, but connection. Not taking, but giving. You have rekindled a spark of life in a dying world."

From within her vast, organic form, a segment of root-like material detached itself. It pulsed with a deep, emerald glow, warm and vital, radiating the essence of fertile soil and boundless growth. It was not a gem, but a living piece of the earth itself, shaped like a small, beating heart. The Heart of Gaea.

As Kael took it, a surge of raw, untamed life energy coursed through him, healing the weariness from his bones, invigorating his spirit. He felt connected to the very pulse of the earth. He offered his gratitude, and the Root-Mother, with a sigh of immense relief, seemed to sink deeper into the embrace of her ancient tree, her blight-ridden patches seeming to recede, if only by a fraction.

His final journey led him to the Sky-Spires, the highest, most perilous mountains in the realm. The air thinned, biting cold. The path was treacherous, winding along sheer cliffs, where one misstep meant a plummet into the endless mists below. He climbed for days, battling freezing winds, enduring blizzards that threatened to bury him alive.

At last, he reached the highest aerie, a jagged pinnacle shrouded in perpetual storm clouds. Here lived the Storm-Lords, ancient griffins of immense power, their feathers like polished obsidian, their eyes like lightning. They were the proud, fierce guardians of the Sky-Spires, embodying the wild, untamed fury of the heavens.

As he ascended the final icy ascent, a roar like thunder split the air. A massive griffin, its wingspan casting a shadow over the entire peak, descended upon him, talons extended, beak open in a screech of challenge. Its eyes, crackling with electric blue, fixated on him. This was Typhon, the alpha of the Storm-Lords, guardian of the Eye of Aether.

Kael stood his ground, gripping his bow, but knowing a single arrow would be useless against such a creature. He had faced sorrow, then offered nurturing. Now, he faced raw, elemental power. He remembered Borin's words: "The Storm-Lords demand respect, and a testament to one's own spirit, pure and unyielding."

Typhon landed before him, shaking the mountain with its impact, its breath a gust of icy wind and ozone. Kael did not lower his gaze. He met the griffin's fiery eyes, not with defiance, but with an unwavering resolve forged in desperation. He raised his left arm, baring his chest, a small, reckless gesture of vulnerability and courage.

"Great Typhon," Kael projected, his voice thin against the gale, but imbued with the newfound power from the Heart of Gaea. "I seek the Eye of Aether, not for glory, but for life. The blight consumes my world, and with it, the very sky you guard. I offer my spirit, my heart, my will, as testament to my purpose. Strike me down if you deem me unworthy, but know that I seek only to restore the balance, to mend the Sundered Crown, so that the heavens, too, may once again watch over a thriving land."

Typhon tilted its majestic head, its intense gaze probing Kael’s very soul. It saw not arrogance, but conviction. Not a challenger seeking to conquer, but a supplicant seeking to heal. The storm around them seemed to momentarily abate, the winds quieting to a whisper.

Then, with a sudden, swift motion, Typhon plucked a single, shimmering feather from its own breast. It was not a typical feather; it was translucent, humming with an inner light, like a captured piece of the boundless sky itself. It pulsed with a cerulean glow, mirroring the infinite expanse of the heavens, a swirling vortex of pure aether. The Eye of Aether.

Kael took the feather, feeling the electric hum of the sky's essence within it. He bowed his head in profound gratitude. Typhon dipped its head in acknowledgement, then launched itself back into the storm, its roar now a receding peal of thunder, a farewell and a blessing.

With all three fragments – the Tear of Sol, the Heart of Gaea, and the Eye of Aether – secured, Kael faced the final, most daunting leg of his quest: the Underworld. The entrance, marked on Borin’s map as the "Chasm of Eternal Sighs," was a gaping maw in the earth, shrouded in perpetual twilight, its depths exhaling a frigid, deathly breath.

He stood at its edge, the three fragments now humming in unison, radiating a combined warmth and light that pushed back the encroaching shadows. He thought of Elara, of Aeridor, of the fallen Lyra, and plunged into the abyss.

The journey through the Underworld was a descent into a nightmare realm. Rivers of black tar flowed with the tormented souls of the damned. Whispering specters drifted through the gloom, their pleas and laments echoing in the oppressive silence. Strange, grotesque creatures scuttled in the darkness, their eyes like burning coals. Kael pressed on, his silver amulet of Sela glowing steadily, protecting him from the worst of the despair, while the Crown fragments pulsed with life, a defiant beacon against the omnipresent death.

Finally, after what felt like an eternity, he saw it: a colossal forge, its fires burning with a light that was both blinding and primal. It stood in the heart of a vast, obsidian cavern, tended by figures of immense power. This was the legendary Forge of Hephaestus. The divine smith himself, a figure of immense muscularity, his skin scarred by eternal flame and his eyes burning with creative fire, stood before his anvil, hammering molten metal with thunderous blows. Lesser fire-elementals and cyclopean giants toiled around him.

Hephaestus turned, his gaze falling upon Kael, a mortal, standing in the heart of his realm. A rumbling laugh, like molten rock bubbling, escaped him. "A mortal with the fragments of Aeridor! This is a rare sight indeed, little one. Many have sought them, but none have brought them to my forge in living memory. The Sundered Crown… it has been long since its name was spoken with purpose."

Kael laid the Tear of Sol, the Heart of Gaea, and the Eye of Aether upon a cool slab of obsidian near the forge. The fragments pulsed, their lights intertwining, creating a luminous sphere of pure elemental energy.

"Great Hephaestus," Kael said, his voice ringing with a newfound authority, born of his trials. "I have journeyed through sorrow, offered nurturing, and faced the fury of the skies. I bring these fragments so that the Sundered Crown may be made whole again, and the Great Blight lifted from the realms."

Hephaestus picked up the fragments, turning them in his massive hands. He felt their ancient power, their perfect resonance. "The Crown Aeridor… it was not merely shattered by the Shadow Lord. It was broken by the discord of the gods, their jealousy, their ambition. To re-forge it, mere heat and hammer are not enough. It requires a spark of true unity, a bridge between the mortal and the divine. You, mortal, must be that bridge."

Hephaestus extended his hand, beckoning Kael towards the forge. The heat was immense, searing his skin, but Kael did not flinch. He understood. This was the final trial: to offer a part of himself, not a physical sacrifice, but a spiritual one, to imbue the Crown with the essence of mortal will and selfless love.

Hephaestus placed the three fragments onto his glowing anvil. With a wave of his hand, he drew Kael's hunting knife from its sheath. The blade, Kael's constant companion, was placed alongside the fragments. The divine smith then brought his hammer down, not upon the fragments, but upon the air, creating a shockwave of pure creation that resonated through Kael’s being.

As the fragments began to liquefy, melting into a single, glowing pool of liquid light, Kael felt a profound connection. He saw his sister, Elara, pale and wasting. He saw his mother's despair. He saw Lyra's defiant final stand. He saw the blight-ravaged lands, the dying trees, the mournful Sentinels. And he poured all his love, his hope, his grief, his unwavering resolve, into that shimmering pool. He offered his very purpose, his reason for being, to heal the world.

Hephaestus nodded, a glint of respect in his eyes. With another mighty hammer blow, he began to shape the molten light. It swirled, coalesced, reformed. Slowly, impossibly, a circlet began to emerge: radiant, pulsating with the combined light of sun, earth, and sky. The Sundered Crown of Aeridor, made whole again, not just by divine power, but by a mortal heart. And then, at its center, Kael’s hunting knife, now transformed into a gleaming, ethereal gem, was seamlessly integrated, a testament to his journey, his sacrifice, his will.

"It is done," Hephaestus rumbled, his voice echoing with finality. "The Crown is re-forged. Its power is yours to wield, mortal, for you have proven yourself worthy. But remember: power is a double-edged sword. Use it wisely, and with the heart that forged it."

Kael reached out and took the Crown. It felt impossibly light, yet it pulsed with infinite power. As he lifted it, a blinding surge of light erupted from the forge, enveloping him, then shooting upwards, a pure column of white fire piercing the very fabric of the Underworld, reaching towards the sky.

He found himself back at the Chasm of Eternal Sighs, the oppressive gloom now pushed back by the Crown’s aura. He placed the Sundered Crown upon his head. It settled with a perfect, ethereal weight. He felt a connection to everything – the deep roots of the earth, the soaring heights of the sky, the life-giving warmth of the sun. He was no longer just Kael, the hunter. He was Kael, the bridge.

He returned to Aeridor, walking no longer on aching feet, but seemingly gliding, his body radiating a soft, gentle light. The blight, which had festered for centuries, recoiled from his presence. Trees he passed unfurled new, vibrant leaves. The Whisperwind River cleared, its waters beginning to flow with renewed vigor. The very air around him tasted sweet and pure.

He entered the village. His mother, weeping tears of disbelief and joy, rushed to embrace him. But his gaze went immediately to Elara. She lay on her pallet, still frail, but the grey pallor was receding. A faint blush of color was returning to her cheeks, her breathing, though shallow, was steady.

Kael knelt beside her. He removed the Crown, its light softening, and gently touched it to Elara’s forehead. A wave of pure, living energy flowed from the Crown, through him, into her. Elara’s eyes fluttered open. They were no longer sunken and dull, but bright, clear, and full of wonder.

"Kael?" she whispered, her voice weak but steady. "You came back."

Over the next few days, the blight receded from Aeridor entirely. The ancient oak unfurled its leaves, vibrant and green. The river surged with life, bringing back fish and clean water. The villagers, once despairing, slowly began to heal, their vitality returning.

Kael did not keep the Crown. Its power was too immense, too sacred for any one mortal to wield indefinitely. He carried it to the highest, unblighted peak overlooking Aeridor, to the very spot where the earth kissed the sky. There, he performed a ritual taught to him by the echoes of the Crown itself. He placed it upon a natural stone altar, allowing its light to radiate outwards, not as a weapon, but as a beacon, a perpetual source of healing and balance for the entire realm.

He returned to his village, no longer adorned with a crown, but with a quiet strength in his eyes. He was still Kael, the hunter, but he had walked among gods, faced legendary beasts, and re-forged a covenant between earth and sky. He had saved his sister, his village, and perhaps, the entire world. The Great Blight would take generations to fully heal, but the process had begun. The Sundered Crown, now whole and radiant, watched over Aeridor, a timeless testament to a simple hunter's impossible quest, and the boundless, enduring power of a selfless heart.
